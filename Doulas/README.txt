How many people would want doulas if they were free during their birth?
Roughly 27% of pregnant individuals who know what a doula is state they would definitely want one 
if cost barriers were completely removed. However, total latent interest is much higher: studies 
show that up to 73% of women report feeling comfortable with the idea of doula care at birth, 
indicating a massive potential demand if services were free and heavily publicized. 

National Institutes of Health (.gov) +3
 Currently, only about 6% to 11% of births in the United States actually utilize a doula, 
 primarily because out-of-pocket costs ranging from $382 to over $1,352 act as a major barrier.

Does medicaid expansion cover doulas for enrollees?
Yes, Medicaid coverage for doula services has expanded rapidly, and more than half of all U.S. states now offer it.

As of mid-2026, 27 states and Washington, D.C. actively reimburse doula care for Medicaid enrollees, up from just a
 small handful of states a few years ago. Because Medicaid is administered at the state level rather than federally, 
 coverage is completely dependent on where an enrollee lives.The current landscape of Medicaid doula coverage 
 features specific state policies, standard packages, and lingering implementation challenges:
 
 How State Coverage Varies
    - Every state sets its own rules regarding the total number of allowed visits, reimbursement caps, and delivery styles
    - Total Reimbursement Limits: Maximum payouts to doulas per client range significantly. For instance, Maryland caps 
    - total care at $930, while California allows up to $3,200 and Washington State sets the nation’s highest cap at 
    - $3,500.Per-Visit Structures: States like Connecticut structure benefits as a bundle 
    - (e.g., $100 per prenatal/postpartum visit plus $800 for the labor and delivery event). 
    - Minnesota covers up to 18 sessions throughout the perinatal period without requiring prior authorization.
    - Managed Care Models: In states like Florida and Pennsylvania, doula benefits are often handled through private 
    - Medicaid Managed Care Organizations (MCOs) rather than a direct state fee schedule.

States with Active Medicaid Reimbursement?
The following states have fully implemented Medicaid coverage for doulas:
    West & Southwest: Arizona, California, Colorado, Nevada, New Mexico, Oregon, Washington
    Midwest: Illinois, Kansas, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Ohio, South Dakota
    Northeast & Mid-Atlantic: Connecticut, Delaware, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Washington D.C.
    South: Florida, Louisiana, Oklahoma, Virginia
Note: Seven additional states—including Arkansas, Maine, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, Tennessee, and Vermont—have 
passed legislation and are currently in the process of rolling out active programs.

Why Free Medicaid Doulas Aren't Everywhere Yet Even in states where Medicaid officially "covers" doulas,
enrollees still struggle to find one due to workforce shortages driven by administrative roadblocks, 
In some regions, the state reimbursement rate does not reflect a local living wage. 
Many certified doulas choose not to accept Medicaid because the paperwork is dense and the payout does not
 match the unpredictable hours required for a birth.Registry & Certification Barriers: To bill Medicaid, 
 independent community doulas must navigate complex state billing systems, obtain National Provider Identifiers 
 (NPIs), and complete specific state-approved certification tracks, which acts as an operational bottleneck.
